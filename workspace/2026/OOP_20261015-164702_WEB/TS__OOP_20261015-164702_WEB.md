## 1. Objective

Deliver a friendly offline course shell for OOPoman in which a student can follow the three-week route, start with the first week, and see persistent progress for its labs and all three week modules in the local Chrome profile. The course structure follows the project's lecture and lab PDFs: weeks 1–3 group lecture/lab numbers 01–03, 04–06, and 07–09 respectively. Week 1 is open; weeks 2 and 3 remain closed.

## 2. Scope

### In Scope

- A welcome page and visible route for three course weeks, with week 1 available and weeks 2–3 visibly locked.
- Three week modules, each based on the corresponding three lecture and three laboratory source files. Lecture pages contain no date or time fields. Filling lecture pages from PDFs and turning labs into practice assignments are reserved for a separate future task:
  - Week 1: lectures 01–03 and labs 01–03.
  - Week 2: lectures 04–06 and labs 04–06.
  - Week 3: lectures 07–09 and labs 07–09.
- Empty theory and practice page shells for week 1. Opening an empty lab records it as viewed/started; it is not completed until a real quiz or practice exists and is completed.
- Per-lab progress for the nine labs and module progress for the three weeks. The overall denominator is 3 modules from the start, including locked weeks.
- Progress persistence in the local Google Chrome profile without registration or login, plus a reset action on the progress page with a standard Cancel/Confirm prompt. Confirm clears only OOPoman progress.
- An updated context diagram reflecting the delivered local site, profile progress, and week availability.
- Real-environment evidence in Chrome for offline start/restart, navigation/locked states, progress persistence, lab start state, and scoped reset. Preserve unresolved H1/H2 observations honestly if the chosen local delivery mechanism cannot satisfy the contract.

### Out of Scope

- Reading, converting, copying, or modifying course PDF content; creating quizzes, practice content, grading, points, or code execution.
- Opening weeks 2–3, or deciding their future content or release dates.
- Authentication, registration, a project-owned server/database, network dependency, deployment, or synchronization between profiles/devices.
- Choosing unapproved grading rules or storing scores.
