## 1. Vision 🔒 FROZEN

Week 2 is available as six clear, self-contained pages: lessons 4–6 and their three practical labs. Each lesson and required lab core fits one hour, keeps object-oriented programming at the center, and leaves deeper source exercises available as optional extension. Students carry out code work on their own computers, compare results with a peer, and use the course page to answer a short quiz, mark the ordinary checklist, see the resulting points, and track completion.

The module opens when all six pages are prepared. Lab scores are calculated from the actual number of quiz questions and checklist items, with 20 points per correct answer or checked item. There is no fixed 500-point target or ceiling; the North Star and course guidance describe the same rule.

**Impact:** Students can continue the course without a gap between its first and second weeks, complete meaningful OOP practice in the agreed time, and understand how each recorded result contributes to their progress.

> *Imagined student quote, illustrative:* “I know which OOP idea I applied, what my partner checked, and why the page shows these points.”

## 6. Definition of Failure (DoF) 🔒 FROZEN

- ❌ 1. The pages introduce a fixed course total, award points for rubric criteria, or calculate points differently from the approved formula.
- ❌ 2. The page content makes bus/tariff details more important than the OOP learning objectives.
- ❌ 3. A required core cannot be completed meaningfully in 60 minutes and the excess work is hidden instead of moved to optional extension or returned as a scope conflict.
- ❌ 4. Module 2 opens before all six pages are prepared, direct routes bypass availability, or module 3 becomes available without authority.
- ❌ 5. Lab completion or points cannot be explained from the quiz answers and ordinary checklist state.
- ❌ 6. Course text promises code execution, server-side checking, or fixed 500-point totals that the local site does not provide.

**On failure:** Keep module 2 unavailable until a complete and coherent Candidate exists. Route a material content/time/scope conflict to the owner; do not silently weaken OOP coverage, alter the scoring contract, or modify the source PDFs.

## 7. Principles 🔒 FROZEN

1. **OOP first** — teach and assess object-oriented design directly; domain stories serve as examples and context.
2. **One-hour complete core** — each required lesson and lab has a meaningful, checkable 60-minute path; further work remains optional.
3. **Visible, dynamic scoring** — points come only from correct quiz answers and checked checklist items, each worth 20; maxima come from actual item counts.
4. **Peer-checked work stays local** — students write and run code on their own computers and review one another outside the site; the page records outcomes but does not claim to inspect code.
5. **Preserve source meaning** — retain supplied PDFs unchanged and represent their material accurately in the HTML pages.
6. **Calm visual hierarchy** — distinguish theory, practice, and assessment with restrained section treatments that improve scanning.
